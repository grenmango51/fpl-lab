# Forecast derivation, version 0.3

This is a component-based statistical model. Its minutes, attacking, bonus, clean-sheet and defensive sub-models are fitted on archived seasons by `python -m backend.fit`, which writes `backend/model_params.json`. Fitting, evaluation and live forecasts share the feature code in `backend/forecast_model.py`. Without that file, the declared version 0.2 rules below apply. It does not use the old international Elo/Dixon-Coles ratings.

## Inputs and time

Official FPL bootstrap provides the current scoring dictionary, squad rules, prices, status and player news. Per-fixture historic xG/xA, minutes, bonus, saves and cards come from official live endpoints and vaastav archives. Season-specific IDs are mapped through stable player `code`. Team expectations use FPL Joe fixture-level market data first, then the directly accessible Elevenify public sheet (or its attributed copy in FPL Joe), then historical team xG estimates. Numeric provider IDs are never treated as FPL IDs.

Archived weekly availability flags come from FPL-Core-Insights `playerstats.csv` for 2024/25 and 2025/26, mapped to stable codes through its `players.csv`. Each snapshot's cumulative points include its own gameweek and never the next one, so the snapshot after GW g is information from before the GW g+1 deadline. Its exact capture time is unknown. A week is used only if at most 10% of players flagged out after it appear in the next gameweek. The downloaded 2025/26 GW1–10 snapshots fail this check (30–41%) and are excluded.

Sources carry UTC extraction time, SHA-256 and a content-addressed compressed raw payload. A failed feed may fall back to its stale cached payload, with an explicit status. Archived payloads and vendor data stay local and ignored by Git. Optional vendor schema failure drops to historical estimates.

## Appearance

Minutes have three states: no appearance, short appearance (1–59 minutes) and long appearance (60+). A fitted multinomial logistic regression gives each fixture's state probabilities. Its 25 role features summarise the player's earlier rows: mean minutes over the last 1, 3, 6, 10, 20 and 38 recorded team fixtures; 60+-minute, appearance and start frequencies over the last 3 and 10; current- and previous-season mean minutes; zero-minute and no-start streaks; sample size; log price; a club change; and position. Twenty of these interact with log lead time (capped at 20 GWs), so recent form gives way to longer-run role at longer leads. The output is selection given the player is available on the day.

This selection model is fitted only on players flagged available at the origin, using origins with a validated archived flag snapshot. Origins without a snapshot are excluded, because an unknown mix of injured players would bias every regular's appearance probability down.

Official flags are grouped into four levels: ≥63% → 75, ≥38% → 50, ≥13% → 25, otherwise out. For a flagged player the role features exclude the current absence spell (trailing zero-minute rows), so they describe the role on return. Each level then has two fitted curves in `(1, log lead, next-GW indicator)`:

- availability `A(level, lead)`, fitted by maximum likelihood with `P(appearance) = A * P(appearance | available role)`;
- a logit shift of the 60+ share of an appearance, because returning players are more often used as substitutes.

On the 2024/25–2025/26 training rows a multiplicative availability fits the observed rates in every role band; an additive flag term in the selection logit did not (it gave flagged-out regulars a 16–23% next-GW appearance chance against about 2% observed). Fitted next-GW availability is roughly 0.6 (75%), 0.34 (50%), 0.09 (25%) and 0.02 (out), recovering with lead.

In live forecasts, an available player uses the selection probabilities directly. For a doubtful or undated injured player, each fixture uses `A(level, lead)` and the shifted split. This replaces the declared official percentage and the 28-day recovery decay for those players. An explicit per-GW availability assumption replaces the flag entirely. Conditional durations are recency-weighted observed means, shrunk by one pseudo-match toward 80 minutes (long) and 20 minutes (short); `xMins = P60 * E[M|long] + Pshort * E[M|short]`.

Declared availability rules still apply where the fit has no evidence. Captured official news with `Expected back D Mon` or `Suspended until D Mon` supplies a planning return date: fixtures before it are unavailable, and fixtures on or after it restore availability (selection probability still applies). Suspensions without an expiry and unregistered/unavailable statuses never recover automatically. Unknown fixture dates keep the current value. Each fixture records the value, its source and the flag level or date assumption. No later news is injected into an archived replay. Without `model_params.json`, the version 0.2 heuristic applies: recency weights `0.5 ** (age_in_matches / 3)` over ten recorded fixtures, a 0.25 zero-appearance pseudocount, the official percentage and `a(t)=1-(1-a_next)*2**(-days/28)` for undated injuries.

Manual minutes set a conditional scenario duration and select the corresponding short/long state. Per-GW schedules accept duration (0–90) and/or an availability assumption (0–1), with each field taking precedence independently. Unspecified duration retains a legacy global duration or the model profile; unspecified availability uses automatic fixture availability. Duration alone does not override availability. Explicit availability is a human assumption. A 0-minute scenario gives 0 points. The same explicit assumption applies to each fixture in a double; blanks stay zero. Components, expected minutes and appearance probabilities are recomputed together.

## Player rates and team adjustment

Each event rate uses up to the 80 most recent appearances (minutes > 0), weighted with a 32-appearance half-life. For an event E (xG, xA, goals, assists, bonus, saves, yellow cards):

`rate90(E) = 90 * (sum(w_i * E_i) + prior90(E, position, price) * 450/90) / (sum(w_i * minutes_i) + 450)`.

The prior is a per-position log-linear function of price, fitted on archived player-seasons with at least 900 minutes and rescaled so its minute-weighted mean is exact. Expensive players therefore start from a higher prior than cheap ones. The 450-minute weight is a declared choice.

Goals per 90 are a nonnegative least-squares combination of the shrunk xG and goal rates, per position. Assists combine shrunk xA, xG and assist rates the same way. Coefficients are fitted on archived fixtures up to three GWs ahead, against actual goals and assists. Fixture expectations multiply the rate by expected minutes and by team goal expectation / the player's historical team xG, clipped to 0.5–2.0.

## Expected points

- Appearance: `2 * P60 + 1 * Pshort`, using the official configured values.
- Goals: expected goals times configured position goal score (including 10 for goalkeepers).
- Assists: expected assists times the official assist score.
- Clean sheets: a logistic function of log expected goals conceded, fitted on archived 60+-minute non-forward rows, times P60 and the configured position score. This approximates full-match CS exposure and does not reconstruct the exact time of each goal.
- Defensive contributions: a per-90 defensive-action rate (16-appearance half-life, 2025/26+ rows only) shrunk by 180 minutes toward the training position rate. The count in each minutes state is negative binomial (dispersion 20) over that state's duration; the expected score is `sum(P(state) * P(count >= threshold))` times the configured score (thresholds 10 DEF, 12 MID/FWD). Keeper contribution is zero.
- Bonus: a per-position nonnegative least-squares fit on the player's bonus rate times minutes, expected goals, expected assists, `CS * P60` and `P60` (keepers: no attacking terms). It is not a reconstructed BPS ranking and cannot yet reflect every 2026/27 BPS change.
- Cards, own goals and penalties: preceding weighted event rates times minutes (yellow cards use the shrunk rate above).
- Conceded-goal deductions: if `N ~ Poisson(lambda)`, `E[floor(N/2)] = lambda/2 - (1-exp(-2*lambda))/4`. The identity follows from `floor(N/2)=(N-(N mod 2))/2` and `P(N odd)=(1-exp(-2*lambda))/2`. Apply this to each minutes state for keepers and defenders.
- Saves: for each minutes state, sum `floor(n/3) * PoissonPMF(n; save_rate * duration/90)`, then weight by the state probability. Numerical summation stops at 44 saves; this truncation is immaterial at observed FPL save rates.

Summing each fixture's components gives a GW forecast. Blank gameweeks are zero. Double gameweeks sum fixture forecasts. A team GW aggregate from a season sheet is never copied onto both fixtures in a double; fixture-specific markets or the historical fallback are used instead.

## Provider anchors and extrapolation

The default live forecast blends the normal public **FPL Form** export for the next five GWs at 50%. IDs, clubs, positions and prices are checked against the current official pool; at least 80% of rows must match. Source values and hashes stay local. Legacy global minutes overrides bypass this automatic provider blend for that player. Per-GW minutes/availability schedules bypass every point and probability blend only in the scheduled weeks, including imported CSVs. A parallel unscheduled forecast passes through the same provider/CSV layers for extrapolation calibration, so a one-week assumption does not change other weeks' provider scales. User CSV projections can form a further weighted layer.

Later anchors now receive more weight: `w_g = 2**(-(last_anchor-g)/2)`. For point-only sources, nonblank base points ≥0.5 identify `r = clip(sum(w_g * provider_points_g)/sum(w_g * base_points_g), 0, 3)`. When a provider supplies appearance probabilities, single-fixture anchors instead compare **points conditional on appearance**: provider points/probability against base points/base probability. Exclude provider probabilities below 0.1 and conditional base points below 0.5 from this rate calibration. Thus a zero injury anchor cannot identify a permanent zero scoring rate. Where the component appearance is zero, a stored conditional event basis allows positive supplied probabilities to restore coherent states; unseen players use the declared 80-minute long-state prior only conditional on a supplied positive appearance probability.

Beyond the final anchor, `a_g = blend_weight * 2**(-(g-last_anchor)/half_life)` (default six GWs). Point-only targets scale the fixture-aware base by `r`. Probability-aware single-fixture targets combine the conditional point ratio with a separate weighted appearance delta, then blend at `a_g`. Only near-fully-available anchors (automatic availability ≥0.95) with positive provider probability identify that future selection delta; temporary injury-zero signals are not carried indefinitely. Direct weeks retain the ordinary point blend. Double-GW points can be blended, but first-fixture source probabilities never alter a double's full-GW state. Blanks remain zero. Weights, thresholds, cap and decay are declared safeguards requiring prospective validation.

The future component forecast carries the fixture schedule, team attack and clean-sheet expectations. This avoids repeating the same short-range point value against every opponent. The scalar ratio is a practical heuristic: it does not identify underlying attacking rates, future minutes, or injury recovery from point totals alone. Both the ratio cap and decay are declared choices needing prospective validation.

The unchanged GW12 benchmark exposed the former next-round availability and zero-base failures. Version 0.2 was developed using that case; replay gains are development results, not a new holdout. The original snapshots are immutable. `tools/replay_forecast.py` reconstructs the exact original source hashes without network/current-cache reads, preserves the original configuration and reports old versus revised forecasts on matching identities. It does not fit weights to Solio.

FPL Form probability changes now update fixture Pplay, P60, xMins, xG/xA and event components together, preserving conditional duration and long/short proportions. An explicit residual reconciles those components with the point forecast. Simulations use the changed appearance states but remain uncalibrated local distributions, not a provider distribution. The historical benchmark remains separate and does not validate the new live availability/anchoring policy.

## Squad scoring and optimisation

Binary variables select owned players, starters, captain, purchases and sales in each gameweek. Integer variables track bank (tenths of millions), free transfers and hits. Constraints enforce 15-player 2/5/5/3 composition, legal XI formations, three per club, a single starting captain, balanced transfers, nonnegative bank, exact hits `max(0, moves - FTs)` and the 5-FT cap. Profit-aware initial sale values follow official half-profit rounding. Prices are held fixed across the horizon.

The objective discounts successive GWs, scores the XI and captain, weights bench points linearly, subtracts transfer-hit points and values saved free transfers. Optional ownership preference is a heuristic, not calibrated relative-rank utility. Candidate screening retains the top 24 and cheapest 8 per position plus held/locked players. Optimality is only relative to that pool; solver status and MIP gap are shown.

Wildcard changes become permanent without using saved FTs. Wildcard and Free Hit preserve the saved FT count; they do not grant an extra transfer. Free Hit creates a temporary squad and then restores the preceding permanent squad/bank. BB counts all 15, TC adds two extra copies of captain points. Chip schedules are supplied by the user. Rebuying and later selling an original player is conservatively valued by the optimiser; server plan validation uses tracked purchase values and independently checks every result.

Displayed team totals include expected autosubs and vice-captain fallback. Under independent player appearances, compute the distribution of missing starters by position, enumerate the eight bench appearance states, and select the first-priority feasible bench subset preserving a legal nominal formation. Conditional bench points are `unconditional_points / P(appearance)`. Reserve goalkeeper contribution is `P(starting keeper absent) * reserve keeper points`. Vice contribution is `P(captain absent) * vice points`, multiplied by two for TC. Any cameo blocks an autosub; double-GW appearance is `1 - product(P(absent in each fixture))`. BB counts all 15 without an extra autosub term.

These are exact expectations **within the independent-appearance approximation**, not calibrated probabilities or a reconstruction of correlated team selection. The MILP objective still uses a linear bench weight and omits nonlinear fallback interactions; its output totals are recomputed with the fuller squad-scoring calculation. The “Best XI” shortcut compares legal formation candidates and bench orders; it is not a full enumeration of every possible XI.

## Strategy sensitivity

The optimiser can run 3–20 seeded scenarios across 1–6 GWs. Each player's points receive a mean-one lognormal multiplier, constant across that horizon, with a user-chosen coefficient of variation. Fixtures, appearance probabilities and prices remain fixed. Forecasts and solver settings are frozen before the background job; reference squad candidates are included in scenario pools. Native solver calls are serialised. Cancellation applies between solves, and partial failures reduce the successful-scenario denominator.

HiGHS also runs with one native thread. A Windows access-violation diagnostic appeared during an earlier concurrent test run despite its exit code being zero; restricting native threads is a mitigation, not a confirmed diagnosis. The subsequent fresh-process full suite and restarted-server background solves completed without that diagnostic. Only SciPy's specific warning that it forwards the `threads` option to HiGHS is filtered; native errors are not suppressed.

First-GW purchase, sale and captain counts describe these sampled assumptions. They are not confidence levels or a fitted uncertainty distribution. Differences use discounted full squad expected points minus hits, without saved-FT utility, relative to the fixed reference strategy under each scenario; a difference can be negative because search/time limits and the linear objective approximate that display metric. Representative strategies can become independently server-validated branches using current normal forecasts. Jobs remain in memory, expose progress and permit JSON export; restarting the server loses them. Browser polling retains the job through temporary connection failures and clears it only after a confirmed missing-job response or terminal state.

## Honest evaluation

`python -m backend.evaluate` refits every sub-model on 2023/24–2024/25 origins (price priors from 2022/23–2024/25) and walks forward through 2025/26 at leads 1, 2, 4, 8 and 12 GWs. Features for an entire GW are created before its outcomes update any player/team state. Historical price features use the preceding observed fixture price. Each origin uses the archived flag snapshot from before its deadline, so only origins GW12–38 qualify. Version 0.2 is scored on the same rows with the same flags and return dates. Assistant managers are excluded.

The main cohort is recent starters (started 2 of their last 3 recorded fixtures before the origin), which depends on neither model. **RMSE is the headline**: expected points are means, and MAE favours predicting the median of a skewed score, which rewards under-prediction. MAE and bias are reported beside it. Appearance probabilities get log loss and Brier scores, overall, by lead and by flag level; a point metric hides them because most flagged rows score zero for both models.

Two decision tests use actual points. Per GW, each forecast picks its best legal squad (2/5/5/3, £100m at origin prices, three per club, legal XI, captain doubled, bench weighted 0.1) and is scored on the actual XI and captain points. Captain choice is scored separately. Differences have a 5,000-replicate gameweek-cluster bootstrap interval; squad and captain totals are noisy, so most decision differences are not distinguishable from zero.

The 2025/26 season informed version 0.3's design choices (flag levels, the multiplicative availability form, role terms), so it is no longer a clean holdout. Only the coming season's prospective captures can confirm the gains. Historical error does not establish competitive accuracy against paid tools. A prospective test must compare immutable forecasts with the same deadline, identical player cohort and the same eventual FPL outcomes.

Prospective capture explicitly bypasses live-source TTLs without deleting valid fallback caches. Component, raw provider and ensemble values use one frozen dataset. Each target receipt records source ages, extraction times, hashes, refetch/content-change flags and required sources. Primary input eligibility requires validated live/cached sources extracted no more than 30 minutes before capture. This is an acquisition policy, not a provider-publication or accuracy guarantee. Unverified CSV extraction time makes the ensemble exploratory. Target availability and origin-export keys remain distinct.

New snapshots preserve every remaining target GW's forecasts, labelled point-layer kind, component projected minutes, manual schedules, fixture membership, official deadline, model version and configuration hash. Raw FPL Form points stay missing beyond supplied weeks; extrapolations belong only to the live ensemble. Legacy next-GW snapshots remain usable with unknown freshness eligibility. Cohorts use captured component xmins ≥45 (legacy ensemble fallback); sparse model coverage is reported, each pair is scored on matching IDs, and all-model cards use the target's available comparison set. Finished weeks with `data_checked=false` are excluded. Missing flags, stale outcome sources and unknown receipts cannot support a primary result.

Lead-time evaluation predeclares `(0,24h)`, `[24h,72h)`, `[72h,168h)`, `[168h,336h)`, `[336h,672h)` and `[672h,∞)` before the frozen deadline. Select the latest valid timestamp per target GW/bin, with filename ties deterministic, before inspecting eligibility or outcomes. A newer exploratory capture does not fall back to an older fresh one. Current deadline changes are disclosed without re-binning; a capture at/after an earlier current deadline is excluded. Pooled MAE, RMSE and bias derive from individual matched player-GW errors, with primary and exploratory sets separate. Player cohorts, configurations and actual leads can differ across bins, and the same actual GW is reused. These descriptive results do not isolate the effect of horizon; a matched cross-bin cohort and GW-clustered uncertainty analysis are needed before a horizon-accuracy claim.

## Player stats scenarios and season optimisation

Per-player, per-GW stat schedules accept unconditional expected goals/assists, a shared per-fixture clean-sheet probability and/or a direct expected-points total. These are explicit human assumptions with an application timestamp and original values, not newly acquired provider statistics. Bounds are declared input safeguards (points −5–60, xG/xA 0–10, CS probability 0–1), not fitted uncertainty limits. Current-season IDs and remaining GWs are validated.

Normal component, provider and CSV forecasting finishes before stat edits. Therefore no stat edit changes the anchor ratios, source-probability calibration or any unedited player-week. In edited doubles, split attacking GW totals using the original nonzero fixture event shares, falling back to expected minutes. No attacking count is allocated to a zero-appearance fixture. The CS probability applies to each fixture and its clean-sheet score is still multiplied by that fixture's P60. Blanks remain zero even if a user supplies a positive assumption. Positive attacking counts or a nonzero point override require a nonzero appearance state; minutes/availability must be edited first.

Existing GW-only provider/imported point residuals are allocated to edited fixtures by expected-minute shares while retaining their component labels. Event edits alter their own goal/assist/CS components. A final direct point edit uses a separately labelled `user_points_adjustment`. Fixture components and GW components then both reconcile with the edited total. Scenario simulations include these explicit point residuals conditional on appearance and remain uncalibrated. Prospective snapshots keep the original component model as a separate baseline and mark affected live-ensemble targets exploratory; manual assumptions and their configuration hash are frozen.

Optimisation now accepts up to 38 requested GWs, clipped at GW38, with one joint multi-period MILP. Weekly ownership locks, ownership exclusions, captain locks and transfer ceilings are real constraints; required players and captains are retained in the candidate pool. A weekly transfer ceiling explicitly supplied for a WC/FH also limits changes that week; otherwise unlimited-transfer chip rules are preserved. The ordinary solve consumes a supplied chip schedule. Requests longer than 14 GWs use the official `highspy` API to initialise the full joint model from a checked incumbent: a bounded four-week transfer solve plus a legal held continuation, with additional seed segments only when later constraints require changes. Every variable/bound/integrality/row constraint is checked before this seed is supplied, and all weeks are free to improve in the subsequent joint search. Original-player rebuys retain the existing conservative sale-value convention.

The requested solve budget is 2–45 seconds with one native thread. Ordinary short requests allocate seed work and model construction within that budget; API forecasting, queueing, native stopping overhead and independent validation add wall time. Long-horizon seed work remains separately bounded by ten segments of at most four seconds, so those requests can exceed the native joint limit. Result status, gap (unknown remains null), candidate/variable/constraint counts and elapsed time are disclosed. The stopping target is a 1% relative MILP gap within the screened pool, and is labelled accordingly. A feasible time-limited result does not establish global optimality, forecast accuracy or Solio proprietary algorithm parity. No-incumbent or contradictory requests are reported as such rather than fabricated. The official Python interface and solution initialisation are documented at https://ergo-code.github.io/HiGHS/dev/interfaces/python/example-py/.

Ordinary short requests with an existing squad now start from a legal held plan and bounded rolling aggregate-point surrogate solves. Each surrogate proposes a squad under the actual budget, sale values, transfer allowance, hits and required players; actual weeks get legal XIs and captain locks. A surrogate may repair an owned-player ban that makes holding illegal. At most one second (40% of a two-second budget) is allocated to this search. The better legal seed is selected using the **unchanged joint linear objective**, validated against the complete joint matrix and passed to `highspy`. The joint search can change every week. Fresh squads and cases without a valid seed retain bounded feasibility search; no universal two-second feasibility guarantee is claimed. Seed lineups avoid expensive autosub enumeration, while displayed results still use the full existing squad scorer. Positive provider points remain eligible for screening when local appearance estimates are zero; screening does not invent new appearance probabilities.

The left quick action uses at most five native-budget seconds for horizons up to 14 GWs, preserving the selected plan's end, right-hand tab, validation and Undo. The full Optimise workspace retains the selected 2/5/15/30/45-second budget. Auto transfers removes the artificial weekly ceiling (up to 15, with normal hit costs). The optional **Max XI points** preset uses decay 1, FT utility 0, bench weight 0 and ownership preference 0: it maximises undiscounted XI + captain − hits within the screened pool. Its objective excludes nonlinear autosubs/vice fallback even though displayed totals include them. This is an explicit planning objective, not Solio's risk/disruption model. [Benchmark and feature audit](SOLIO_OPTIMISER_BENCHMARK.md).

A legal held squad now also supplies the checked incumbent for each short seed segment through the official `highspy` interface. The small segment budget can therefore search for improvements from an existing feasible plan. Segments whose weekly ownership/exclusion constraints require changes cannot use an illegal hold; they retain ordinary feasibility search. The final joint model and objective are unchanged, and a time-limited solve can still return the supplied incumbent without improving it.

The separate WC/FH placement job freezes all provider, CSV, minutes and stat inputs once, then compares a no-added-chip baseline with deterministically screened legal weeks for one unused chip in one season half. Each placement gets a fresh joint transfer/lineup solve over identical GWs and initial permanent holdings, prices, bank, FTs and objective settings. Prior source-plan decisions and manual chips stay fixed. Complete plans are independently replayed for FPL legality, permanent Free Hit restoration, transfer prices and full squad EV; weekly ownership/captain/transfer limits are checked again. Rankings use discounted full squad expected net points without FT utility or ownership preference, with undiscounted totals alongside. A failed baseline cannot produce an improvement recommendation. The bounded date sample, candidate screening, native MIP limits and independent-appearance approximation do not establish global optimality or paid-model parity. Cancellation and wall-budget limits take effect between solves, with explicit skipped-candidate states. [Request and result contract](CHIP_STRATEGY_CONTRACT.md).

For strategy jobs, short windows also receive a constraint-checked held-squad incumbent through `highspy` when all requested weekly constraints permit one. The two-second minimum can then return that feasible plan if native search produces no improvement, including legal WC/FH state and FT preservation. When neither a hold nor a surrogate can satisfy the required future changes, bounded native feasibility search can still fail. The joint objective and forecast assumptions remain unchanged.

## Observed Solio points

The user-authorised `use_solio` setting defaults to true. The normal public JSON report supplies selected next-GW `prPoints` cells, not a complete five-week matrix. Match accent-normalised player names, official club abbreviations and positions (including Solio GKP → local GK); retain current IDs and stable player codes. Reject ambiguous names, conflicting duplicate values, nonfinite/non-numeric points, unknown GW/deadline receipts and future generation times. The supplied deadline must equal the official target deadline. A report past its deadline, older than a declared eight-hour publication-age safeguard, or acquired from a stale/unavailable source does not replace forecasts.
